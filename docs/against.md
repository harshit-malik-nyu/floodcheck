# The case against this analysis

The strongest argument that none of it should change what anyone does.

---

## 1. No real docket has been touched

Every number here comes from arithmetic on published constraints or from blocs
generated by code in this repository. The sampling ceiling is a property of
hypergeometric sampling. The signal separation is measured against synthetic
campaigns I wrote. The cost asymmetry is list prices divided by a documented
rate limit.

**That is the most serious objection and it is not fixable from here.** The
collector exists, refuses to fabricate, and has never been given a key. Until
it runs, this is a careful argument about a system nobody has looked at.

## 2. The synthetic generators encode what I expect

The "careful" adversary paces irregularly, pauses overnight and draws names
from the full space — because those are the three things the signals test. An
adversary I did not anticipate would be caught or missed for reasons this
design cannot see.

This already went wrong once. The first careful generator drew names from a
pre-generated pool that was *less* diverse than the lawful campaign's, so it
earned a flag that measured my generator rather than its behaviour. There may
be more of that.

## 3. The lawful campaign is a caricature

Real advocacy campaigns have organisers who batch submissions, members who
share a laptop, forms that timestamp at receipt rather than at sending, and
international members. Any of those could produce the signatures this flags as
machine-like.

**The false-positive rate measured here is therefore a floor.** On real data it
would be higher, and the one lawful campaign in five that already trips a flag
suggests the margin is thin.

## 4. "Metadata is 250× cheaper" assumes metadata carries the signal

The sampling ceiling says metadata reaches campaigns two orders of magnitude
smaller. It does not say metadata *identifies* them. The index returns a title,
a date and an agency — the richer fields used in `signals.py` come from the
per-comment endpoint, which costs one request each, exactly like the text.

So the 250× advantage applies to duplicate-title and timing analysis, and
**not** to the name and geography signals that do most of the separating. The
README states the ceiling and the signals adjacently and does not make that
distinction sharply enough.

This is the objection I would press hardest after argument 1.

## 5. The cost model prices the wrong attacker

It assumes an attacker who pays list price for tokens and submits through the
ordinary channel. A serious one uses a cheaper model, batches, and may have
other ways in. The attacker's cost is therefore an overestimate, which makes
the asymmetry **worse** than shown — but it means the specific figures are not
the ones a real adversary faces.

The auditor's side is also crude: $75 an hour is an estimate, and an agency
with staff already reading comments has a different cost structure than an
outside investigator.

## 6. A one-cent fee is modelled as if it has no other effects

The fee result is arithmetic on the attacker's cost curve. It says nothing
about the people who would not comment if comment cost money, which is the
entire reason the channel exists. Presenting it beside the detection results
invites a reading the analysis cannot support, and the README's disclaimer may
not be enough to prevent it.

## 7. The framing assumes flooding works

The argument presumes a docket's comment volume changes what an agency does.
Agencies say repeatedly that comments are not votes and that identical
submissions carry no extra weight. If that is true in practice, a flood that
nobody can detect may also be a flood that does not matter.

The counter is that volume creates political pressure through press coverage
and congressional attention regardless of legal weight — but that is an
assertion, and nothing here measures it.

---

## What survives

- **The sampling ceiling** is arithmetic. A perfect detector needs two members
  of a campaign in its sample, and that bounds what any budget can reach.
- **Duplicate rate does not distinguish lawful from fabricated.** Three blocs
  with identical duplicate rates and different provenance is a construction, not
  a measurement, and it is enough to show the statistic cannot carry the weight
  placed on it.
- **The cost asymmetry's direction.** The specific dollars are disputable; that
  writing scales with money and checking scales with wall clock does not depend
  on any of them.

## The objection I cannot answer

Argument 1. Everything here is a well-formed argument about a system I have not
observed, and the one thing that would fix it is a key the collector has never
received.
